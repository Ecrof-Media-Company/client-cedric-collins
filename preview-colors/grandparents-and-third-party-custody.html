Grandparents and family who step in.

Sometimes the people keeping a child steady are grandparents, aunts, uncles, or close family. Ohio law gives them limited but real options, and Cedric helps them use those options carefully.

Companionship and visitation

Ohio law lets grandparents and other relatives ask the court for companionship or visitation in certain situations, including during a divorce or legal separation, after a parent has died, and when the child’s parents were never married. The court decides based on the child’s best interest, and it gives real weight to the parents’ wishes.

Third party custody

Parents have a strong legal right to raise their own children. For a grandparent or other nonparent to be awarded custody, the court generally needs to find that a parent is unsuitable, for example because of abandonment, inability to care for the child, or a situation that would be harmful to the child.

These cases are serious and fact specific. As a court appointed Guardian ad Litem, Cedric has seen how courts weigh what is best for a child, and he brings that perspective to families who are stepping in.

FAQ

Common questions.

Do grandparents have automatic visitation rights in Ohio?+

No. Grandparents must ask the court, and only in certain situations. The court decides based on the child’s best interest and considers the parents’ wishes.

Can I get custody of my grandchild?+

Possibly, but Ohio law favors parents. A nonparent usually needs to show that a parent is unsuitable. Cedric can review your situation at a free consult.

The child already lives with me. Do I need a court order?+

Often it is wise. Without an order you may have trouble enrolling the child in school, consenting to medical care, or keeping stability if a parent changes their mind.
