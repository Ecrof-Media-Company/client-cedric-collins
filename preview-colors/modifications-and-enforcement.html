Changing an order (post decree modifications)

Custody, parenting time, child support, and in some cases spousal support can be changed after the original order. What it takes depends on what you want to change:

  • Custody. Ohio courts generally look for a change in circumstances since the last order, and for the change to be in the child’s best interest.
  • Parenting time. Schedules can be adjusted as children grow, schools change, or a parent moves, based on what is best for the child.
  • Child support. A recalculation that differs by more than 10 percent from the current amount is generally treated as a change worth revisiting.
  • Spousal support. It can be modified only if the original decree allowed the court to keep that power.

Enforcing an order (contempt)

A court order is not a suggestion. When the other parent keeps denying parenting time, stops paying support, or ignores what the decree says, you can ask the court to enforce it through a motion for contempt.

Depending on the situation, a court can order make up parenting time, payment of what is owed, fines, attorney fees, or other consequences. Cedric will help you document what has happened and ask for a remedy that actually changes the pattern.

FAQ

Common questions.

How soon after a divorce can I change custody?+

There is no fixed waiting period, but courts expect a real change in circumstances since the last order. Cedric can tell you at a free consult whether your situation is likely to meet that standard.

My ex keeps missing support payments. What can I do?+

You can work with your county child support enforcement agency and you can ask the court to hold the other parent in contempt. The right path depends on how much is owed and for how long.

Can we change the order if we both agree?+

Often, yes. Agreed changes still need to be approved by the court to be enforceable, so put the agreement in writing and have it filed.
